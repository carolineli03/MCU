ABOUT THOSE STORY DATES.
Marvel has officially dated the timeline up to roughly Endgame — that's where the Iron Man-is-set-in-2010 placement comes from, which surprises people. Anything after the Blip is reconstructed from on-screen clues and the odd interview, so treat late-Phase-Five placements as best-guess rather than gospel. A handful of titles sit in other universes entirely and have no fixed MCU date at all.

    Nine storylines

    The Infinity StonesSix rocks, eleven years

    The spine of the whole Infinity Saga. Each stone is introduced casually, years before anyone explains what it is.

    1. Captain America: The First AvengerThe Tesseract turns up in a Norwegian church. Nobody calls it a stone yet.
    2. ThorThe Ether sits in Odin’s vault, unremarked, in a single background shot.
    3. The AvengersLoki’s sceptre and the Tesseract, both weaponised. Two stones on screen, still unnamed.
    4. Thor: The Dark WorldThe Aether is finally named as one of six. This is the one plot thread that redeems the film.
    5. Guardians of the GalaxyThe Orb is opened and the rules get explained out loud for the first time.
    6. Avengers: Age of UltronThe sceptre cracks open. The Mind Stone becomes Vision’s forehead.
    7. Doctor StrangeThe Time Stone hangs around Strange’s neck, and he learns to abuse it immediately.
    8. Avengers: Infinity WarThanos collects five of them in one film. You have seen every one before.
    9. Avengers: EndgameThe heist revisits the exact scenes above. Watching in order is why it lands.
    Steve and BuckyThe longest friendship

    Two boys from Brooklyn, seventy years of interference, and the shield changing hands twice.

    1. Captain America: The First AvengerBucky falls from the train. Steve goes into the ice believing he is dead.
    2. Captain America: The Winter SoldierBucky is alive, brainwashed, and the reason S.H.I.E.L.D. is a lie.
    3. Captain America: Civil WarThe 1991 footage. Tony finds out who killed his parents, and the team ends.
    4. Avengers: Infinity WarWakanda gives Bucky his mind back, just in time to lose him again.
    5. Avengers: EndgameSteve chooses the past. Sam gets the shield, and Bucky watches him take it.
    6. The Falcon and the Winter SoldierSam earns the title publicly. Bucky works through the ledger.
    7. Thunderbolts*Bucky ends up leading a team of people who used to be the problem.
    The multiverse and the TVAHow everything got complicated

    The rulebook for Phases Four to Six. Skip Loki and the next dozen titles stop making sense.

    1. Avengers: EndgameThe time heist creates a branch nobody cleans up. This is the inciting incident.
    2. Loki — Season 1The TVA explains branching timelines, variants, and who has been pruning them.
    3. Spider-Man: No Way HomeStrange’s botched spell pulls in villains from two other film franchises.
    4. Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of MadnessIncursions introduced. Universes can now kill each other.
    5. Ant-Man and the Wasp: QuantumaniaKang proper, exiled in the Quantum Realm, warning about his variants.
    6. Loki — Season 2Loki takes over holding the whole thing together. The single most load-bearing finale.
    7. Deadpool & WolverineThe Void and the TVA absorb twenty-four years of Fox X-Men films into canon.
    8. The Fantastic Four: First StepsEarth-828 is a fully separate universe, presented without apology.
    9. Avengers: DoomsdayWhere all of the above is supposed to collide.
    Wanda and VisionA grief that keeps escalating

    The most emotionally consistent character arc in the MCU, and the one that most needs the TV shows.

    1. Avengers: Age of UltronWanda and Pietro volunteer for HYDRA experiments. Vision is born and Pietro dies.
    2. Captain America: Civil WarWanda and Vision are quietly falling for each other while the team splits.
    3. Avengers: Infinity WarShe has to kill him herself. Then Thanos undoes it and kills him again.
    4. WandaVisionThree weeks later she takes a town hostage inside her own grief.
    5. Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of MadnessThe Darkhold turns the grief into a body count. Requires WandaVision, absolutely.
    6. Agatha All AlongThe spin-off picks up the leftovers, including her son.
    7. VisionQuestWhite Vision, no memories, going looking for them.

    1. Iron Man 2A world map on Tony’s monitor has a flagged location exactly where Wakanda sits. Blink and it is gone.

    Peter ParkerThree films, one long punishment

    Common questions

    What order should I watch the Marvel movies in?

    Release order for a first run — the reveals were built to land in that sequence. Story order re-sorts everything by when it happens, which is better for a rewatch but defuses half of Endgame. This guide does both, and remembers what you have watched.

    How long does it take to watch the whole MCU?

    About 58 hours for the essentials, roughly 132 for the recommended path, and about 211 hours for every film, series and special. At two evenings a week that is a little over a year for the full run.

    Do I need to watch the Disney+ shows?

    A few matter: WandaVision, both seasons of Loki, and Daredevil: Born Again all feed directly into later films. Most of the rest are optional, and this guide marks which is which.

    Where do the X-Men fit into the MCU?

    Fox’s X-Men films were a separate universe until Deadpool & Wolverine, where the TVA pulls Deadpool and Logan out of it and into the MCU. The original cast — Patrick Stewart, Ian McKellen, James Marsden, Rebecca Romijn and Alan Cumming — return for Avengers: Doomsday.

    What should I watch before Avengers: Doomsday?

    The Fantastic Four: First Steps and Thunderbolts* are the on-ramps, Loki season 2 explains the multiverse rules, and Deadpool & Wolverine sets up the X-Men crossing over. The original X-Men films are worth knowing but not required.
